The physics & chemistry inside
E = ρ·g·h·η·V
Hydro-electricity — physics. Energy from falling water: density × gravity × dam head × turbine efficiency × the volume released. Release more in winter → more power, but that water never reaches the summer fields.
C = M / V
Salinity — chemistry. Concentration = dissolved-salt mass ÷ water volume. Evaporation and irrigation remove fresh water; the salt stays. As a sea shrinks, C climbs — how the Aral went from ~10 g/L to over 70, and the fish died.
C ≤ Csat → salt falls out
Saturation & dust — chemistry. Water can only hold so much salt. Past saturation the extra salt precipitates onto the drying bed as a white crust — and that exposed salt is what the wind lifts into the Aralkum's toxic dust storms.
dust → lungs
The human cost. That dry bed is not just salt: decades of cotton left DDT and defoliant residue in it. The storms carry the salt-and-pesticide dust over ~2M people downwind in Karakalpakstan — driving respiratory disease and killing a 60,000-job fishery. The chemistry ends in a clinic.
Honest note. Upstream is an educational systems model on realistic, published-order numbers — it shows how the tradeoffs are shaped, it is not a validated forecast of any real year.
Two rivers (Syr Darya ≈37, Amu Darya ≈79 km³/yr), 1960 sea ≈1,064 km³ / 68,000 km² / 10 g/L; measured area, volume & salinity 1960–2010; 1987 N/S split; 2005 Kok-Aral dam; 2014 eastern basin dried; ~60,000-job fishery; ~2M people in the Karakalpakstan dust shadow; DDT/defoliant residue in the dust.
